01Overview

Auracast is the Broadcast feature of Bluetooth LE Audio. One Transmitter streams audio to any number of nearby Receivers, such as headphones, hearing aids or speakers, without pairing each of them.

At OpenEcore we built the devices behind it: the Transmitter that sends the Broadcast, the Receiver that listens to it, and the Assistant device that helps Receivers find and join a stream. This includes a 4-channel Transmitter that can handle either 2 stereo or 4 mono audio streams from a single unit, and an earbuds prototype.

The Transmitter uses the NXH3675 chipset for its Bluetooth LE Audio radio. It takes four digital channels over I2S/TDM and encodes them with the LC3 codec at 48 kHz with 10 ms frames. One Broadcast carries up to four streams, so a single Transmitter can serve two languages in stereo or four mono channels. The Receivers are built as TWS earbuds or a stereo speaker, and the same design was also verified with an STM32WBA55 microcontroller as the Receiver. I scaled the audio path from mono to four channels.

02How it works

  • Audio sourceI2S / TDM · 4 channels
  • LC3 encoderCompresses the audio
  • TransmitterBLE Audio Broadcast
  • ReceiversDecode and play out

  1. Audio comes in

    Audio enters the Transmitter over I2S/TDM. TDM carries several channels on one data line, which is how the path grew from mono to four channels.

  2. LC3 encoding

    The LC3 codec compresses the audio for Bluetooth LE with low latency and low power.

  3. Broadcast

    The Transmitter sends one Broadcast with a name, language tags and an optional PIN. Each language is a subgroup of left and right streams, for example English on streams 1 and 2 and French on streams 3 and 4. No pairing is needed, so any number of Receivers in range can listen.

  4. Find and join

    A Receiver joins through a phone's built-in stack (tested on a Samsung S24) or through the SkyAmour Connect app. It syncs only to the left and right streams of the chosen language, decodes the audio and plays it.
